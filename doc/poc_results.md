## Set-up

| | |
|---|---|
| Target | electricity, **site** level, kWh per half hour; scored on observed values only |
| Sites | **91** of 134 (≥ 85 % observed in both train and test windows, ≥ 1 year of history, not heavily imputed) |
| Issue time | **16:30 local on D−1**; forecast the 48 half-hours of D (lead 7.5–31 h) |
| Known at issue | demand up to 16:30 D−1 (no settlement lag assumed); Agile prices for D; NESO day-ahead ND and embedded forecasts; the **00z IFS ENS run of D−1** for forecast weather |
| Train / test | train ≤ 31 May 2025; test 1 Jun 2025 → 8 May 2026, one origin every 5 days (69 origins, all weekdays represented) |
| Baselines | seasonal naive (same half-hour one week earlier); profile quantiles (last 8 same-weekday/slot values) |
| SARIMA | dynamic harmonic regression per site: log demand = 3 weekly Fourier harmonics + SARIMA(1,0,1)(0,1,1)₄₈ errors, re-estimated at every origin on the 6-week context (warm-started from a fit on the last 8 training weeks), gaps left to the Kalman filter, Gaussian quantiles on the log scale; 6,279 fits on 36 cores |
| Zero-shot | Chronos-2, TimesFM 3.0: 6-week context up to the issue time, no covariates, quantile heads |
| Foundation models + covariates | the same contexts plus the known-ahead covariates as past/future series: local calendar (half-hour, weekday, weekend, bank holiday), the site's Agile price, NESO day-ahead ND/embedded/peak forecasts, and weather (2 m temperature, dewpoint, wind): ERA5 over the context in both variants, ahead either ERA5 (**+ ERA5**, perfect weather) or the ensemble mean of the 00z IFS ENS run of D−1 (**+ IFS ENS**, forecast weather). Chronos-2 takes them natively; TimesFM 3.0 as standardised past+future covariate rows; TabPFN-TS as extra columns |
| TabPFN-TS | TabPFN v3 time-series checkpoint on the published TabPFN-TS feature table (running index, calendar sine/cosine terms, auto-detected seasonal terms; 42 features, 56 with covariates), one regression per site and origin (8 estimators) |
| LightGBM | global direct multi-horizon quantile models (q10/q50/q90) on calendar, lags/aggregates ending at the origin, price, NESO day-ahead covariates, site statics; three weather variants: none / ERA5 at target time (perfect weather, upper bound) / IFS ENS control + ensemble mean/spread |
| Metrics | MAE, normalised MAE (MAE / site mean), CRPS approximated on the 9-quantile grid, 80 % coverage; skill = 1 − metric/metric(seasonal naive), median across sites |

### Reading

- **Every learned model beats seasonal naive by 26–35 % MAE and 38–49 % CRPS** on the median site; 97–99 % of sites improve.
- **Zero-shot foundation models lead, and known-ahead covariates add a further 0.7 pt of nMAE**: Chronos-2 12.2 → 11.5 %, TimesFM 3.0 12.4 → 11.6 %; TabPFN-TS gains most from them (14.1 → 12.0 %) because its own feature table carries no weather. LightGBM gains 0.3–0.6 pt.
- **Forecast weather is as good as perfect weather at day-ahead range**: the IFS ENS variants match the ERA5 variants to within 0.1 pt for every family. The 0.25° nearest-grid weather is the same for hospitals in one city (242 of 275 sites share a cell with another), which limits what any weather covariate can add.
- **The weather ensemble adds nothing to the day-ahead distribution**: control member, ensemble mean and the 51-member mixture score identically (nMAE 11.5 %, CRPS 5.13–5.14). Member medians differ by 0.6 % of mean demand on the median site-day (90th percentile 2.3 %), far below the model's own quantile width. The member spread is nevertheless a strong *error indicator*: MAE of the member mean rises from 3 % to 30 % of mean demand across spread deciles (`ensemble_spread_skill`).
- **SARIMA (dynamic harmonic regression) is a weak point forecaster here**: better than naive in pooled MAE at every lead, but worse on the site-averaged nMAE (18.9 %) because it fails on small, erratic sites; its wide Gaussian bands reach 82 % coverage by width, not skill. 61 % of the 6,279 warm-started refits converged within 30 iterations. A stationary specification (daily and weekly Fourier terms, no seasonal differencing) is the fix to try before drawing conclusions.
- **Calibration**: TimesFM 3.0 is calibrated (81 %), Chronos-2 slightly narrow (75–76 %), LightGBM narrow (73 %), TabPFN-TS 77 %. Per-site recalibration is the cheapest CRPS gain available.
- Error peaks at 21 h lead (≈ 13:30 on D, the daytime plateau) for every method.

## Reconciliation

Exact summing matrix over 218 nodes (101 meters of the 91 sites, sites, trusts, regions, national). Point forecasts of every node are mapped through S G; the variance-based methods use only the base forecasts' errors at earlier origins (expanding window) and all methods are scored on the same 61 origins after an 8-origin warm-up. nMAE (%) for Chronos-2 + IFS ENS base forecasts (LightGBM + IFS ENS in brackets):

| Approach | meter | site | trust | region | national |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base (direct, incoherent) | 11.4 (12.9) | 11.4 (13.0) | 8.3 (10.1) | 8.4 (9.8) | 8.0 (9.2) |
| Bottom-up | 11.4 (12.9) | 11.4 (12.9) | 8.1 (9.7) | 7.9 (9.2) | 6.9 (7.7) |
| WLS (variance) | 11.5 (13.0) | 11.5 (12.9) | 8.1 (9.6) | 7.7 (8.9) | 6.8 (7.6) |
| MinT (shrinkage) | 12.1 (13.6) | 12.0 (13.5) | 8.6 (10.4) | 8.2 (9.8) | 7.2 (8.6) |
| WLS (structural) | 23.4 (27.6) | 22.7 (26.1) | 9.2 (10.9) | 8.7 (9.8) | 7.0 (7.8) |
| Middle-out (trust) | 27.6 (26.9) | 27.3 (26.7) | 8.3 (10.1) | 8.0 (9.4) | 7.0 (7.9) |

- **Variance-scaled WLS is the best coherent set for every model**: it keeps the bottom levels and beats bottom-up at every aggregate (national 6.8 % against 8.0 % direct).
- **MinT with a full shrinkage covariance is worse than its diagonal cousin**: 218 nodes against at most ~2,900 past error rows leaves the cross-covariance poorly estimated. Training-period residuals would give it a fair chance.
- **Anything that ignores node variance (OLS, structural WLS) or imposes recent shares (top-down, middle-out) damages the bottom levels badly** (22–51 % nMAE at meters): scales differ by four orders of magnitude.

## Caveats

- No settlement lag: half-hourly data assumed available at 16:30 D−1. Set `data_lag_periods: 48` to emulate next-day delivery.
- Scoring counts every observed half-hour, including days that look like partial outages (RH801, RXN02); a suspected-outage filter and a sensitivity table are the next step.
- IFS ENS 6-hourly steps are linearly interpolated to half-hours; dewpoint/radiation exist only from March 2024; radiation is not used as a foundation-model covariate (accumulated in IFS, hourly in ERA5).
- CRPS is a 9-quantile approximation; LightGBM's three fitted quantiles are interpolated onto that grid; bottom-up and reconciled forecasts are point-only.
- One test window (Jun 2025 – May 2026), origins every 5 days, no retraining inside it; no significance tests yet.
- SARIMA: 39 % of refits hit the iteration cap; TabPFN-TS uses 8 estimators and a 6-week context.
